## Interpretation limits

V4 holds λ fixed, while V5 permits independent re-tuning per checkpoint. If selected strengths
differ, V5 is not a fixed-strength comparison. Neither protocol selects a site on
these confirmation data. A lower Good→Bad target rate or higher Bad→Good rate is not sufficient
for improved selective steering: inspect nontarget pickup, intact behavior, controls and return.
Calibration endpoints are measured immediately at the intervened site, not after downstream nonlinearities.
Joint decoded vectors may be signed; no clipping or simplex projection is added. Controls use
the existing relative-SVD-threshold 1e-6 numerical decoder null space and match norms on their
own current histories. V5 null dimensions/orthogonality diagnostics and fitting row counts are
recorded in each checkpoint JSON. Decoder R² is not evidence of causal belief use.

## Results and verdict

All 24 V5 representation/direction/checkpoint tuning selections use the λ=1
fallback: none met its criterion. Thus the realized comparisons also hold λ
fixed, despite allowing independent re-tuning. All update-29 V5 confirmation
cells exactly match historical v5, and all update-29 V4 site-0 cells exactly
match historical v4. The newly evaluated V4 sites use the same held-out seed.

The clearest improvement is the primary agent under the V4 nuisance-controlled,
calibrated protocol at early sites. At site 0, pooled Good→Bad pickup falls
0.1452→0.0836 and Bad→Good rises 0.6860→0.9372. Nontarget Good pickup is
0.9633→0.9555 / 0.9072→0.9631 (Good→Bad / Bad→Good); nontarget Bad pickup is
0.0100→0.0063 / 0.0327→0.0112. The target null controls retain high Good and low Bad
target pickup (Good 0.9163→0.8754, Bad 0.0152→0.0112), rather than producing
comparable target flips, although their nontarget behavior is not invariant.
Primary site 1 also improves both target directions, 0.3389→0.0642 and
0.5306→0.8015, with nontarget Good pickup 0.9606/0.9587 at update 316.
Both primary early sites meet the descriptive pooled ≤0.10/≥0.5 thresholds
at frozen λ=1. This is not perfect selectivity: intact nontarget Good pickup
remains about 0.98, and not every rock is strongly suppressed. At primary
site 0, Good→Bad rock-index 6 remains 0.315 and rock-index 4 worsens
0.027→0.147; Bad→Good reaches at least 0.812 for every rock.

The early-site result is not uniform across agents. At V4 site 0, run 1 seed 2
has weaker Good→Bad suppression (0.1438→0.3686), but higher Bad→Good pickup
(0.4526→0.5650) and much better Bad→Good nontarget Good pickup
(0.8089→0.9577). Run 2 seed 2 worsens in both target directions at site 0:
0.1127→0.2591 / 0.6895→0.4999. Its unrounded Bad→Good estimate is 0.4998605,
below 0.5. At site 1, both target directions improve on all three agents;
however run 2 seed 2 loses nontarget Good selectivity (Good→Bad
0.9367→0.9037; Bad→Good 0.8619→0.7750), and run 1 seed 2 still has
Good→Bad pickup 0.3004. Sites 2/3 show mixed target changes with continued
disruption; for example primary V4 site-2 Bad→Good pickup falls
0.3319→0.2451, nontarget Good falls 0.7113→0.6441, and return falls
-13.65→-43.33. A lower Good→Bad return by itself is not a selectivity failure:
successful suppression intentionally forgoes reward from a Good target.

The plain V5 recipe still does not meet either pooled criterion at the paper's
site on any agent. Update-316 marginal Good→Bad/Bad→Good pickup is
0.2826/0.2575, 0.3288/0.1879, 0.2751/0.1770 (primary, run 1 seed 2,
run 2 seed 2). Joint is 0.3214/0.2437, 0.3064/0.0753, 0.3814/0.0995.
Good→Bad remains above the uniform-random confirmation baseline 0.1967;
Bad→Good remains below 0.5. Some target effects grow, especially marginal
site 1, but they do not amount to consistently more selective steering.
At primary site 2, marginal Good→Bad nontarget Good pickup drops
0.5610→0.3361, and the control target pickup at update 316 (0.2800) is
essentially the same as steered (0.2826). Joint does not consistently beat
marginal, and its null-control family also changes as the fitted decoder
null space changes.

Longer training improves joint linear decoding at every site on all three
agents: paper-site joint R² rises 0.6017→0.8057, 0.5926→0.8189,
0.6218→0.7654. V4 paper-site mean per-rock marginal R² rises
0.883→0.950, 0.884→0.949, 0.867→0.937. V5 marginal scores remain
heterogeneous; primary paper-site rock-index 5 declines 0.160→-0.127.
These prediction improvements do not establish improved causal accessibility
or identify the cause of the intervention changes.

**Verdict:** update 316 is substantially more steerable for the primary agent
under the calibrated nuisance-controlled protocol at blocks 1 and 2. There is
also a cross-agent directional target improvement at block 2, but with uneven
selectivity. Longer training does not uniformly improve block-1 steering across
agents, does not rescue the paper-faithful marginal/joint recipe, and does not
produce a uniformly selective seven-rock belief flip. These are matched-seed,
checkpoint-specific behavioral results from three continuation lineages, not
independent training replications or proof of an internal Bayesian model.
